# The research behind Rep Coach's coaching

In the app, "Ask why" on the plan, any exercise or any set shows the rule that produced it, the facts it used and the rows below, and lets you question Claude about it. The rules engine (`core/`) and the instructions Claude plans from (`ClaudeCoach.SYSTEM_PROMPT`) follow
the peer-reviewed findings below. Each rule names its source in the code. Where the evidence is thin,
this page says so, and the rule is labelled as coaching practice.

| Rule in the app | What the evidence says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| At least 10 hard sets per muscle per week; the coach tops up muscles below that. Sets count as half for muscles that only assist. | Gains in size and strength rise with weekly sets, with diminishing returns that come sooner for strength. Fractional counting (assisting muscles = 0.5) fitted the data best. ≥10 sets/week outperformed fewer. | Pelland et al. 2026; Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 |
| Three full-body sessions a week, so each muscle is trained 2–3 times. | Each extra training day added about 3% strength gain; frequency barely affected growth once volume was equal. Twice a week beat once a week for growth. | Pelland et al. 2026; Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2016 |
| A muscle that did 3 or more hard sets in the last 48 hours isn't trained hard again. The next full-body day trains the recovered muscles instead; if most of the body is still recovering, the plan says rest and offers only an easy session (2 sets, more in reserve, same weights). Each exercise says when its muscle was last trained. | After sets taken to failure, strength and speed took 24 to 48 hours or more to recover; sets stopped short of failure recovered faster. Training on back-to-back days gave the same strength and size gains as spacing sessions 48–72 hours apart when weekly volume was equal, so the rule is about not repeating hard work on the same muscle, not about avoiding consecutive days. The 3-set threshold is coaching practice. | Morán-Navarro et al. 2017; Yang et al. 2018 |
| Reps by exercise: heavy compounds 5–8, machine and dumbbell compounds 8–12, single-joint work 10–15, side and rear delts, calves and abs 12–20. Each exercise card says why. | Muscle growth is similar across a wide range of loads (about 5 to 30 reps) when sets end near failure; heavier loads produce more maximal strength. | Schoenfeld et al. 2017 (load); Lopez et al. 2021 |
| Rest: 2:30 for compounds, 2:00 for accessories, 1:30 for isolation. | Resting more than 60 s gave a small growth benefit, with no clear gain past 90 s. In trained men, 3 min rest beat 1 min for strength and size. | Singer et al. 2024; Schoenfeld et al. 2016 |
| 2 reps in reserve on compounds and 1 on isolation, plus 1 more on low-energy days. | Growth improves as sets end closer to failure while strength barely changes. Training all the way to failure gave no meaningful extra growth. | Robinson et al. 2024; Refalo et al. 2023 |
| Reps in reserve as the effort scale; each set is re-planned from how hard the last one felt. | The RIR-based RPE scale is a valid way to rate and adjust set difficulty during a session. | Helms et al. 2016 |
| Double progression: add 2.5 kg once every set reaches the top of the rep range. | Increase load by about 2–10% when the lifter can beat the target reps, using the smallest practical step. | ACSM 2009 |
| When several big lifts stall, suggest a lighter week (about half the sets, same weights, more in reserve) rather than time off. | Coaches agree deloads should cut volume and effort, planned or as needed. A full week off slightly reduced strength gains in trained lifters, with no effect on size. | Bell et al. 2023; Coleman et al. 2024 |
| Under 6 hours of sleep or a resting heart rate 5+ bpm above your average counts as one point less energy. | Acute sleep loss lowered performance by about 7.6% on average, more for afternoon and evening sessions. | Craven et al. 2022 |
| Support exercises for a stalled lift (for bench: close-grip, triceps, upper back). | **Coaching practice, not tested directly.** It rests on the general finding that strength gains are specific to the movement trained. | — |
| Plateau = under 1% gain in estimated 1RM over 8 weeks; estimated 1RM uses the Epley formula. | **Rule of thumb.** Epley's formula is widely used and reasonably accurate under about 10 reps, but it comes from a coaching manual rather than a peer-reviewed study. | — |
